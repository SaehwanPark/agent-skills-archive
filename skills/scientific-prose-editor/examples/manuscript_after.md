# Introduction

Improved risk stratification remains a clinical research goal, but the value of the laboratory marker for 30-day mortality prediction in hospitalized adults is uncertain. We evaluated its association with 30-day mortality.

# Methods

In this illustrative retrospective cohort, we included 412 adults discharged from a hypothetical hospital between 2018 and 2020. We used logistic regression to estimate the association between the marker and 30-day mortality, adjusting for age and sex. These methods were selected to explore potential associations, not causal effects.

# Results

Among 412 adults, the adjusted odds ratio was 1.42 (95% CI 1.11–1.81). This association suggests that the marker may contribute predictive information, although its incremental performance was not evaluated.

# Discussion

The observed association motivates evaluating whether this marker adds predictive value beyond available clinical information. The retrospective design and the absence of an incremental-performance analysis limit conclusions about clinical use.
