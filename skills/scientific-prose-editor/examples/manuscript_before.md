# Introduction

Risk stratification is a pivotal and multifaceted area of investigation. It plays a crucial role in modern clinical practice. It is important to note that there remains an increasing need for improved prediction. Previous studies have failed to address this problem. We evaluated whether a laboratory marker was associated with 30-day mortality in hospitalized adults.

# Methods

In this illustrative retrospective cohort, we included 412 adults discharged from a hypothetical hospital between 2018 and 2020. We used logistic regression to estimate the association between the marker and 30-day mortality, adjusting for age and sex. These methods were selected to explore potential associations, not causal effects.

# Results

Results were very promising. Among 412 adults, the adjusted odds ratio was 1.42 (95% CI 1.11–1.81). The results show that the marker could be useful for predicting 30-day mortality.

# Discussion

These findings underscore the importance of this work and highlight the need for further research. Future research is needed to better understand this complex and multifaceted issue.
