# Genre and section profiles

These are reader-oriented editing defaults, not authorship markers. The target outlet's instructions supersede generic guidelines.

## Manuscripts

- **Abstract**: establish purpose, design/population, key estimates, and bounded conclusion. Honor structured/unstructured requirements; do not invent values or infer clinical utility from discrimination metrics.
- **Introduction**: articulate the verified gap and decision relevance without overclaiming previous work's deficiencies. Avoid repeating broad disease burden without making it specific.
- **Methods**: prioritize operational precision, reproducibility, variables, models, assumptions and time alignment. Passive voice and repeated terminology can be necessary. Do not substitute synonyms for technical terms.
- **Results**: report observed estimates, uncertainty and populations before interpretation. Preserve figure/table labels, subgroup qualifications and multiplicity cautions. Do not turn exploratory analyses into prespecified ones.
- **Discussion**: open with the actual contribution, distinguish prior evidence from results, calibrate limitations, and make implications specific to the study. Avoid generic “transformative” conclusions or unsupported clinical recommendations.
- **Conclusion**: answer the study question at the evidence's demonstrated level, not a generic exhortation for more research.

## Grants

- **Specific Aims**: preserve aims, hypotheses, milestones, feasibility and substantive investigator decisions verbatim in meaning. Parallelism and repeated aim formats are often necessary.
- **Significance**: connect a documented problem, knowledge gap, decision, population, and feasible contribution; maintain justified urgency. Do not invent epidemiologic burdens or preliminary evidence.
- **Innovation**: describe verifiable methodological or substantive differences rather than novelty adjectives. Do not fabricate “first” claims.
- **Approach**: maintain rigorous design, sample-size assumptions, pitfalls, alternatives, analytic methods, and success criteria. Avoid replacing details with generic assurances of rigor.
- NIH requires particular care: applications/sections substantially developed by AI do not become acceptable simply because the prose is edited; refer to NIH guidance and institutional rules.

## Research proposals

- Preserve research questions, assumptions, proposed methods and decision gates. Distinguish planned vs completed work.
- Make key dependencies visible. Do not claim access to data, feasibility, approval or preliminary findings that have not been established.
- Avoid generic problem–solution templates when a specific uncertainty is known, but keep useful proposal structure.

## Technical writing

- Protect APIs, CLI syntax, mathematical notation, architecture, pseudo-code, exact identifiers and test specifications.
- Prefer consistent identifiers and explicit conditions over varied synonyms. Paragraph and sentence parallelism is a feature when contrasting variants or algorithms.
- Distinguish observed benchmark results from design aspirations, expected behavior and assumptions.

## Voice preservation

- Sample nearby author-written text when legitimately provided and permissioned; infer register, sentence length, use of “we”, directness and domain terminology.
- Preserve disciplined uncertainty. Do not create fake imperfections, misspellings, colloquialisms or erratic syntax to mimic “human” writing.
- The author may prefer restrained positive framing; accuracy and calibrated claims still apply.
